# Style Guide Editing

Good editing serves the reader and keeps the author's meaning. Change what
the style asks for and nothing else.

## 1. Find the style

In order of preference:

1. A style guide the user gave: in the message, an attached file, the
   project's instructions or a file in the workspace (look for names like
   `style`, `guide` or `voice`).
2. A sample of their writing they want matched.
3. Otherwise the house style below.

Also settle the audience, the purpose and the format (email, report, web
page, release notes), and any length limit.

## 2. House style

- Plain words: "use", not "utilise"; "about", not "approximately".
- Short sentences, one idea each. Active voice unless the actor doesn't
  matter.
- Lead with the point. The first sentence of each section says what it is.
- Specific beats vague: numbers with units, dates, names of things.
- Headings in sentence case. Lists when items are parallel; prose otherwise.
- One term per thing. Don't switch between "customer", "client" and "user".
- Numbers: one to nine in words, 10 and up in figures; always figures with
  units (5 km, 3%).
- Dates written out unambiguously: 4 March 2027, not 04/03/27.
- No filler ("it is important to note that"), hedging stacks ("might
  possibly"), or cheerleading.

## 3. Edit in passes

1. **Structure**: is the main point first? Does each section earn its place?
2. **Clarity**: unclear sentences rewritten; jargon explained or cut.
3. **Concision**: repetition and filler removed.
4. **Consistency**: terms, capitalisation, numbers, dates, tense.
5. **Correctness**: spelling, grammar, punctuation, and facts flagged (not
   changed) when they look wrong.

## 4. Hand it back

- The edited text in full, ready to paste.
- Then **What changed**: three to six bullets on the kinds of change, and
  any place you were unsure of the author's meaning. Quote the original
  where it helps.
- If the user wants to review each change, give a list of
  `before -> after` pairs instead of the full text.

Never add claims the author didn't make. If a fact needs checking, say so
under **What changed** instead of rewriting it.
