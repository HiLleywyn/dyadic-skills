# Meeting Action Items

People read a meeting summary to learn three things: what was decided, who
does what by when, and what is still open. Lead with those.

## Steps

1. **Read everything first.** Notes are out of order and people change
   their minds halfway through. The last word on a topic wins.
2. **Find the decisions.** A decision is something the group agreed and
   won't revisit without a reason: "we'll launch in two regions first". A
   preference or an idea is not a decision.
3. **Find the actions.** An action has a task, an owner and ideally a date.
   - Use the owner's name as written in the notes.
   - If nobody took it, write **Unassigned**. Never guess an owner.
   - Dates: copy what was said ("by Friday"). Turn it into a date only when
     the meeting's date is known, and say which day you took it to be.
4. **Find open questions**: things raised and not settled, and who will
   bring the answer if anyone said so.
5. **Write the summary** in the format below. Keep the wording of the notes
   where it matters (numbers, names, commitments).

## Format

```
**Summary:** two or three sentences on what the meeting was for and where it landed.

**Decisions**
1. ...

**Action items**
| Owner | Action | Due |
|---|---|---|
| Name | Verb-first task | date or "not set" |

**Open questions**
- ... (who will follow up, if anyone)
```

Then, only if asked, a follow-up message to the attendees: a greeting line,
the decisions and action table, and a closing line. No sign-off clichés.

## Rules

- Never invent a decision, owner, date or number.
- Mark anything you inferred: "(inferred from the discussion of the budget)".
- Keep confidential asides out of the follow-up message unless asked.
- If the notes are too thin to tell, say what is missing.

## Example (invented)

From notes saying "Mara will send the vendor shortlist next week; Tobin
thinks we should wait on the pilot, nobody objected to starting in April",
the action table gets `Mara | Send the vendor shortlist | next week`, the
decisions get "Start the pilot in April" only if the notes show agreement,
and Tobin's point goes under open questions if it wasn't settled.
